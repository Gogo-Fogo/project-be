# Grid alerts: early warning for blackouts

Your **utility** (the company on your bill) tells you about outages on your street. Your **grid
operator** runs the wider grid and warns when supply gets tight, often hours or a day before
rolling blackouts. Grid-operator alerts are about **grid stress**, whatever the cause: heat,
cold, power plants offline, or an attack. They don't work once the internet is down, so they're
for getting ready, not for during the outage.

## Find your grid operator (US)

Check your state on the operator's own map. They change rarely.

| Operator | Area (roughly) | Alerts |
|---|---|---|
| **PJM** | Mid-Atlantic + parts of the Midwest (13 states + DC) | **PJM Now** app → More → Alerts |
| **ERCOT** | Most of Texas | ERCOT app / TXANS alerts (ercot.com) |
| **CAISO** | Most of California | Flex Alert sign-up, Today's Outlook (caiso.com) |
| **MISO** | Central US, Great Lakes to Louisiana | Notifications on misoenergy.org |
| **NYISO** | New York | Notifications on nyiso.com |
| **ISO-NE** | New England | **ISO to Go** app |
| **SPP** | Central plains | Notifications on spp.org |
| None of these (much of the Southeast and West) | | Your utility's own alerts |

**Outside the US:** your national grid operator usually has alerts too. Examples: National Grid
ESO (Great Britain), AEMO (Australia), RTE's Ecowatt (France). Ask your agent to look up the
one for your country.

**For everyone:** your utility's outage texts, your county or city alerts, and the phone's
emergency alerts.

## Which alerts to turn on

The names differ by operator, but the rule is the same:

- **ON:** anything with **Alert, Warning, Emergency, Load Shed / Load Dump, Voltage
  Reduction, Conservation Appeal or EEA** in the name.
- **OFF:** **prices, markets, drills/tests, reserves, transmission postings.** These are
  routine and would spam you.
- **Region:** your state/zone plus the whole system, if offered. Not every state.

## The usual ladder (North America; PJM names as the example)

| Step | Alert | Means | Do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hot / Cold Weather Alert | Extreme weather tomorrow; the grid will be stretched. | Charge the power station, phones, power banks. |
| 2 | Max Generation / Load Management Alert (day ahead) | Every plant will be needed tomorrow. | Plus: car half full, water jugs full, check the cards and radio. |
| 3 | (Pre-)Emergency Load Management Reduction Action | **Now:** big customers are being paid to cut power. | Cut your own use (no laundry or gaming, heat/AC a few degrees). |
| 4 | Maximum Generation Emergency Action | All plants flat out, buying from neighbours. | Everyone home or reachable; fill the bathtub; eat from the fridge first. |
| 5 | Voltage Reduction Warning / Action | Voltage lowered ~5%; lights may dim. | Unplug sensitive electronics or run them from the power station. |
| 6 | Manual Load Dump / Load Shed | **Rolling blackouts**, usually 30–60 min per area. | Switch to the blackout plan: radio, lanterns, cards, comms plan. |

**NERC EEA 1 / 2 / 3** are the North America-wide names. EEA 3 means blackouts are imminent or
happening.

## Perspective

- Steps 1–3 happen several times a year and rarely lead to blackouts: "charge up", not "panic".
- Rolling blackouts are rare but real. Texas, February 2021: millions of homes, for days.
- A sudden local outage (storm, accident, substation attack) can come with **no** grid alert.
  That's what the rest of the kit is for.

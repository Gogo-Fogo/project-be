EMERGENCY CARDS

Call 911 if you can. Written Nov 2026 from American Red Cross, American Heart Association, USDA, CDC and EPA public guidance. Not a substitute for a first-aid/CPR class.

CHOKING

If they can cough hard or talk: let them keep coughing. Stay with them.
If they CANNOT cough, talk or breathe: CALL 911 (or have someone call), then:

ADULT or CHILD over 1 year
  1. Stand behind them. Lean them forward.
  2. Give 5 firm back blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
  3. Give 5 abdominal thrusts: fist just above the belly button, other hand over it,
     pull sharply IN and UP.
  4. Repeat 5 back blows + 5 thrusts until the object comes out or they pass out.
  Pregnant or very large person: do the thrusts on the center of the chest instead.

BABY under 1 year
  1. Lay baby face-DOWN along your forearm, head lower than chest, support the jaw.
  2. Give 5 back blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
  3. Turn baby face-UP, head lower than chest.
  4. Give 5 chest pushes: 2 fingers in the center of the chest, just below the nipples,
     about 1.5 inches deep.
  5. Repeat until the object comes out or baby passes out.
  NEVER do belly thrusts on a baby. Don't sweep the mouth blindly.

IF THEY PASS OUT (any age)
  Lay them on their back and start CPR (see the CPR card). Each time you open the
  mouth for breaths, look for the object and take it out only if you can SEE it.

CPR (someone collapsed and isn't breathing normally)

  1. Tap and shout. No response and not breathing normally (gasping doesn't count)?
  2. CALL 911 (or have someone call). Ask someone to find an AED if there is one.
  3. Lay them on their back on something firm.

ADULT (and teenager)
  - Hands stacked, heel of hand in the CENTER of the chest.
  - Push HARD and FAST: 2 to 2.4 inches deep, 100-120 pushes a minute
    (the beat of "Stayin' Alive"). Let the chest come all the way back up.
  - If you are trained: 30 pushes, then 2 breaths (tilt head back, lift chin,
    pinch nose, 1 second each, watch the chest rise). If not trained: just push, no breaths.

CHILD (1 year to puberty)
  - One or two hands, about 2 inches deep, same speed. 30 pushes, 2 breaths.

BABY (under 1 year)
  - 2 fingers just below the nipple line, about 1.5 inches deep, same speed.
  - 30 pushes, then 2 small puffs covering baby's mouth AND nose.

KEEP GOING until they breathe, help takes over, or an AED is ready.
AED: turn it on and do exactly what it says. Keep pushing while it charges.
Swap with someone every 2 minutes if you can; tired pushes are too shallow.

SEVERE BLEEDING

Spurting, pooling, or soaking through cloth = life-threatening. CALL 911.

  1. PRESS. Put a clean cloth (or your gloved/bagged hand) right on the wound and
     press HARD with both hands. Don't lift it to look.
  2. Soaked through? Put more cloth ON TOP and keep pressing. Don't remove the first layer.
  3. Deep wound in the thigh, armpit, neck or groin: stuff (pack) cloth INTO the wound,
     then press hard on it for at least 3 minutes.
  4. ARM or LEG still bleeding badly with hard pressure? TOURNIQUET:
       - 2-3 inches ABOVE the wound (toward the body), never on a joint.
       - Real tourniquet, or a 2-inch-wide strap/cloth plus a stick: tie, twist the stick
         until the bleeding STOPS, then tie the stick in place.
       - It will hurt. That's expected. Do NOT loosen it.
       - Write the time it went on (on their skin or the tourniquet).
  5. Keep them lying down and warm (blanket). Nothing to eat or drink.

Small cuts: press for 10 minutes, rinse with clean water, cover.
Get help if it won't stop, is gaping, is from an animal or rusty object, or shows
infection later (spreading redness, heat, pus, fever).

BURNS

  1. Stop the burning: away from the heat. Clothes on fire: STOP, DROP and ROLL,
     or smother with a heavy blanket.
  2. COOL it under cool (not ice-cold) running water for 20 minutes. Even a while
     after the burn, it still helps. No ice, no butter, no toothpaste, no oil.
  3. Take off rings, watches and clothing near the burn before it swells,
     unless it's stuck to the skin (then leave it).
  4. Cover loosely with plastic wrap (lay it on, don't wrap tight) or a clean,
     non-fluffy cloth.
  5. Don't pop blisters.
  6. Pain: ibuprofen or acetaminophen (paracetamol) as the box says.
  7. Keep the person warm otherwise; big burns make people cold fast.

CALL 911 / GET HELP if:
  - the burn is bigger than their palm, or deep (white, leathery or charred),
  - it's on the face, hands, feet, genitals or a joint, or goes all the way round a limb,
  - it's from electricity or chemicals (chemicals: rinse 20+ minutes first),
  - they breathed in smoke (cough, hoarse voice, soot around nose/mouth),
  - it's a baby, a small child or an elderly person.

HEART ATTACK and STROKE

Both: CALL 911 right away. Note the time it started. Don't let them drive.

HEART ATTACK signs
  - Chest pressure, squeezing or pain lasting more than a few minutes, or coming back.
  - Pain spreading to arm(s), back, neck, jaw or stomach.
  - Short of breath, cold sweat, sick to the stomach, lightheaded.
  - Women, older people and diabetics often have NO chest pain: just breathlessness,
    tiredness, nausea, back or jaw pain.
  What to do
  1. Call 911. Have them sit and rest, loosen tight clothing.
  2. If they are not allergic to aspirin and have no bleeding problem: have them
     CHEW one regular aspirin (325 mg) or 4 low-dose (81 mg).
  3. If they collapse and aren't breathing normally: start CPR (see the CPR card).

STROKE signs: think BE FAST
  B  Balance: sudden dizziness or loss of balance
  E  Eyes: sudden trouble seeing
  F  Face: one side droops when they smile
  A  Arm: one arm drifts down when both are raised
  S  Speech: slurred or strange
  T  Time: call 911 NOW and note the time
  Do NOT give aspirin, food or drink for a stroke. Lay them on their side if they're drowsy.

CARBON MONOXIDE (CO): the outage killer

You can't see or smell it. Many outage deaths are from CO, not cold.

NEVER use indoors, in a garage, or near open windows:
  - generators (keep at least 20 feet / 6 m from the house, exhaust pointing away),
  - charcoal or gas grills, camp stoves, propane heaters not made for indoor use,
  - a car running in a garage, even with the door open.
Never heat the home with the oven or stove.

SIGNS: headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea/vomiting, chest pain, confusion,
sleepiness. Several people (or pets) sick at once in the same place = think CO.

IF YOU SUSPECT CO
  1. Get EVERYONE (and pets) outside into fresh air NOW. Don't stop to open windows.
  2. CALL 911 from outside.
  3. Someone passed out and not breathing normally: CPR (see the CPR card), outside.
  4. Don't go back in until the fire department says it's safe.

A battery CO alarm near the bedrooms is the best protection. Test it monthly.

FOOD IN A POWER OUTAGE

KEEP THE DOORS SHUT. Every opening lets the cold out.

  Fridge:  keeps food safe about 4 HOURS if unopened.
  Freezer: about 48 HOURS if full, about 24 HOURS if half full (unopened).

After the power comes back
  - Fridge food (meat, fish, eggs, milk, soft cheese, leftovers, cut fruit) that was
    above 40°F (4°C) for more than 2 hours: THROW IT OUT.
  - Freezer food that still has ice crystals or is 40°F (4°C) or colder: safe to refreeze or cook.
  - Never taste food to check it. Smell and looks don't tell you if it's safe.
  - WHEN IN DOUBT, THROW IT OUT.

Usually fine without the fridge for a while: hard cheese, butter, whole fruit and veg,
bread, peanut butter, jam, ketchup, mustard, opened canned fruit juice.

Tips
  - A thermometer in the fridge and freezer tells you for sure.
  - Coolers with ice/frozen packs can save the most important food (meds, baby food).
  - Medicines that need cold (insulin etc.): check the leaflet; most insulin is fine at
    room temperature for weeks.

(Normal storage times like "1-7 days in the fridge" do NOT apply once the fridge is off.)

SAFE DRINKING WATER

Best first: use stored bottled water. Plan 1 gallon (4 liters) per person per day.

BOIL (best method)
  1. Cloudy? Let it settle, then pour it through a clean cloth or coffee filter.
  2. Bring to a ROLLING boil for 1 minute (3 minutes above 6,500 ft / 2,000 m).
  3. Let it cool. Store covered in clean containers.

BLEACH (if you can't boil)
  Use plain, UNSCENTED household bleach (6% or 8.25%). Not "splash-less", not scented.
                   6% bleach         8.25% bleach
    1 liter        2 drops           2 drops
    1 gallon       8 drops           6 drops
  Double it if the water is cloudy or very cold. Stir, wait 30 MINUTES.
  It should smell slightly of chlorine; if not, add the same dose again, wait 15 min more.

Won't fix: water with fuel, chemicals or flood water from industrial areas.
Boiling and bleach kill germs, they don't remove chemicals. Find another source.

Hidden water at home: water heater tank, ice cubes, canned fruit/veg liquid.
NOT from: toilet bowl, radiators, waterbeds, pools (for drinking).
